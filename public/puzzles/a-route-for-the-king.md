White to move.
Black has just played ...gxh4. Recapturing looks natural, but White has a stronger idea.
How can White create a winning route into Black's position?

## Explanation

47.Ng2! allows Black to capture again: 47...hxg3+ 48.Kxg3. This temporary pawn sacrifice allows the king to enter the position as quickly as possible. The knight can then return through f4, while the king is able to move through the newly accessible kingside squares.

After the tempting 47.gxh4, White's own pawn occupies h4 and blocks this path. Black can defend then with ...Bg6 and ...Bf7, making it much harder for White's king to break through this position. Preserving the pawn is less valuable than gaining access to the opponent's weak squares.

In the game, 48...Ke6 49.Nf4+ Kf5 50.Nxh5 recovered the pawn, and White later advanced the king through g4 and h5 to g6. The positional gain is created by an active king working together with the knight against a restricted bishop.

This position comes from game 9 of the 1984 World Championship in Moscow. Karpov won, although later inaccuracies gave Kasparov further drawing chances.
