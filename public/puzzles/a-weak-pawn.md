White to move.
Black's king and h8-rook are boxed in, but the advanced e6-pawn is under pressure.
If white can protect it, it will become a lasting advantage.

## Explanation

24.Qxe5! fxe5 25.Rf1 sacrifices the queen for a knight to open the f-file. White's bishop on h6 controls f8, and the pawn on e6 controls f7. The threat of Rf8 mate ties Black's pieces to the defense of the king.

White has only a rook and two bishops against a queen and two rooks, but none of Black's material can move without creating a losing disadvantage; The h8-rook is trapped, the king has no legal move, and the other pieces must keep the mating threat under control. This gives White time to improve the bishops and advance the queenside pawn majority.

Gusev won this game against E. Auerbach in the 1946 championship of the Molniya Sporting Society.
