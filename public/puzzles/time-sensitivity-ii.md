Black to move.
Black has two extra pawns, but White is ready to bring the king to the center and establish a blockade.
How can Black get there first?

## Explanation

e4 is the critical square that we want to bring the king to. However, a passive move allows white to get there first and establish a blockade. 

47...Bh3! offers the bishop to clear the king's path with tempo. After 48.gxh3 Kf5 49.Kf2 Ke4, Black's king reaches the center before White can establish the blockade. Black has surrendered a bishop, but the active king and passed pawns provide a winning position.

An ordinary retreat such as 47...Bc2 allows 48.Kf2 Kf5 49.Ke3, stopping the king from entering e4 and creating a drawn position. The natural 47...Be4 obstructs Black's own king. This sacrifice makes White spend a move capturing instead of bringing the king closer.

Declining the sacrifice also requires an answer to its threat against g2. For example, after 48.Kf2 Kf5 49.Ke3, Black can play 49...Bxg2, creating a third passed pawn on the kingside. White cannot keep that pawn while establishing the same blockade as after an ordinary bishop retreat.

In Topalov-Shirov, Linares 1998, the game continued 50.Bxf6 d4 51.Be7 Kd3 52.Bc5 Kc4 53.Be7 Kb3, and White resigned. The king's entrance into white's position lets Black coordinate the advance of both separated passed pawns.
